# 04 — Growth, retention, distribution and business

No plan can guarantee a viral hit. This plan optimizes for understandable value, voluntary return, useful sharing and sustainable production. All targets below are proposed pilot decision rules, not observed metrics or industry benchmarks.

## 1. Growth thesis

The launch loop is: a clear explanation earns trust → a fan shares the explanation → a recipient experiences the same value without friction → some return for the next weekend → their questions improve future coverage.

The product must work alone before it requires a community. One source-linked story can be useful with ten readers. A public discussion network with ten users is mostly empty and creates moderation responsibilities immediately.

### What “hooked” should mean

- “This helps me understand a race, so I choose to return.”
- “I can trust the distinction between confirmed and inferred.”
- “This widget makes the schedule convenient.”
- “This is worth sending to my friend.”

It should not mean anxiety, notification saturation, streak punishment, outrage bait, misleading cliffhangers or a feed that never ends. Include caught-up states, pause controls and an easy exit.

## 2. Activation hypotheses

Candidate activation event: within the first seven days, a visitor opens a reviewed moment, completes its explanation or evidence exploration, and voluntarily saves/follows/shares or returns for another moment. This is a hypothesis to validate against later race-weekend retention, not a proven causal definition.

Measure individual candidate behaviors separately before freezing a composite metric. Reading completion is approximate; dwell time is not proof of comprehension. In pilot interviews, ask the user to explain the moment in their own words.

Primary funnel:

```text
Useful shared/search entry
  → first explanation
  → evidence or deeper context
  → save/follow/share (optional)
  → return for next covered race weekend
```

No signup is required at any step. Do not define all anonymous readers who decline tracking as failures; measurement coverage must be shown.

## 3. North star and guardrails

Candidate north star: **weekly fans completing a useful explanation journey**, reported alongside the share of the consented/measurable cohort. A qualifying journey combines a content interaction and an explicit useful signal such as evidence inspection, a save, or an optional “this explained it” response. Prevent multiple refreshes from inflating counts.

North star is paired with race-weekend return rate, comprehension checks, substantive correction rate and editorial hours per useful publication. It is not used alone to claim PMF.

Race-weekend return definition: among first-time activated users during covered weekend N who can be measured with consent, proportion returning during the next covered weekend N+1. Show numerator/denominator, calendar gap, source segment and consent coverage. Also inspect return over the next three covered weekends and off-week story consumption. Do not equate a summer break with product churn.

## 4. Return loops

| Loop | Trigger | Value | User investment | Return reason |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weekend companion | Chosen session reminder or visit | Schedule and brief | Follow team/spoiler preference | Next session/weekend |
| Understanding | Confusing incident | Evidence-linked explanation | Save concept/case | Similar incident later |
| Strategy learning | Curated pit decision | Try choice, reveal trade-off | Optional learning progress | New challenge |
| Upgrade tracker | Followed team development | What changed and what is unknown | Follow team/component | Later deployment/performance evidence |
| Historical discovery | Off-week interest | Original story with context | Save collection | Next chapter/topic |
| Creator sharing | Insight worth communicating | Clear source-bearing card | Edit share message | Recipient feedback/new questions |

Personalization starts with explicit interests and editorial relevance. A simple deterministic ranker can use freshness, chosen entities, source quality, content diversity and unseen status. Avoid inferred sensitive traits, opaque emotional profiling and optimizing solely for click probability.

## 5. Sharing specification

Trigger: after the user finishes a useful explanation, compares a scenario or opens evidence—not at first launch. Use a small inline share action, not a blocking modal.

Outputs: canonical link, spoiler-safe 1200×630 social card and optional vertical original diagram/card. Those dimensions are output-format choices; verify each channel's current requirements at implementation. Every card carries event/date, a short finding, status, source attribution and link. Do not copy broadcast stills without permission.

Share flow: tap Share → preview/correct spoiler choice → native share sheet/copy link. Target at most three intentional interactions. User chooses recipient/platform and sends; the product never auto-posts or imports contacts.

Suggested copy: “This explains why the pit decision was difficult—includes the tyre/traffic assumptions.” Do not fabricate a user's personal experience in prefilled copy; make it editable and clearly a draft.

Recipient route: open exact moment → useful summary → sources → optional next explanation → optional follow/install. No generic signup landing page, forced redirect to an app store or requirement to install to read.

## 6. Referral economics, without imaginary virality

Launch uses useful content sharing, not cash rewards or an affiliate program. Current referral rate, margins and willingness-to-pay are unknown. Therefore reward budget is zero; do not launch credits, contests or promised lifetime benefits.

Track share initiation separately from actual sends and actual arrivals. Native share sheets usually cannot establish that a person sent a message. A copied link is not an invitation delivered. Avoid presenting raw share-clicks as K-factor.

Illustrative model only:

- 1,000 activated fans × 20% who share × two genuinely reached recipients per sharer × 25% recipient activation = 100 referred activations.
- Effective K = 0.10 referred activations per activated fan per defined cycle.
- These inputs are made-up scenarios for reasoning, not forecasts. Generation time, overlap, recipient duplication and churn matter.

Measure attribution through consent-appropriate first-party campaign/content tokens, not personal names, emails or cross-site fingerprinting. Prefer coarse creator campaign links at launch. Report unattributed arrivals rather than inventing a conversion path.

Only consider rewards after retention, fraud controls, contribution margin and rights are known. If introduced, reward a genuine retained activation/purchase after a defined validation period, cap liability, publish terms and avoid pay-to-win or gambling mechanics. Affiliate partnerships require disclosed commercial relationships and separate agreements.

## 7. Zero-ad-spend acquisition plan

### A. Original explainers

One editorial effort creates a useful web explanation, one original chart/card and a short text summary linking back. Repurposing changes presentation, not factual claims. Share where self-promotion is allowed and participate genuinely. Do not carpet-bomb subreddits, Discord servers or replies.

### B. Search

Start with a small set of excellent concept and historical-case pages: undercut/overcut, pit loss, tyre degradation, track limits, racing room, safety-car strategy and interpreting telemetry. Each concept links to an actual worked case and method. Avoid thousands of thin generated “driver versus driver” pages.

Technical requirements: crawlable static HTML, stable canonical URLs, descriptive titles, accurate authorship and update times, sitemap, internal related links, useful 404s and structured data only when content genuinely fits. No fake reviews, mass doorway pages or guaranteed ranking claims. [Google's people-first content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content).

### C. Small creator collaborations

After a working dossier exists, identify five relevant small creators or campus motorsport clubs. Offer a concrete original diagram or source-backed explainer they can inspect, with a useful link and clear attribution/permissions. Personal outreach is proposed, not authorized or sent by this plan. Do not buy shout-outs before retention evidence.

### D. Widget-led discovery

Show the real widget solving a schedule problem, then demonstrate the explanation it opens. Widget screenshots are not the whole pitch because this category already has established competitors. Use verified app screenshots and explain offline/freshness behavior.

### E. Open development

Publish selected methodology, synthetic fixtures and reproducibility notes if appropriate. Keep restricted data and credentials private. A public GitHub project can establish engineering credibility, but stars are not consumer retention.

## 8. Editorial launch package

Before inviting strangers, prepare:

- One polished race dossier with ten connected moments.
- Two reviewed incident explanations, including an ambiguous/no-finding example.
- Two strategy explanations with explicit assumptions.
- One upgrade dossier where uncertainty is honestly preserved.
- Three evergreen learning pages.
- Two historical stories with checked citations and clip links.
- About, methodology, source policy, corrections, privacy and contact/report issue pages.
- Three honest demo videos using our own UI/assets, not unlicensed race footage.

This is a quality target, not a requirement to rush 20 weak articles. Start inviting the initial research cohort with one complete dossier; finish the package before a wider release.

## 9. Race-weekend and off-week programming

| Window | Content | Solo budget rule |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-weekend | Schedule + three things to understand | Reuse verified evergreen explanations |
| Practice/qualifying | One reviewed observation if evidence exists | Do not claim full live coverage |
| After race | Five key moments and coverage limitations | Publish after checking, not by a speed promise |
| Following days | One incident or strategy deep dive | Choose based on genuine user questions |
| Off-week | One Legends story or learning challenge | Batch evergreen work when feasible |

Initial editorial capacity assumption: 3–5 hours/week, more during a selected race weekend. Log actual time. If consistently exceeded, cover fewer events, publish fewer briefs or recruit a reviewer; do not sacrifice correctness to maintain cadence.

## 10. Notification and messaging discipline

No push backend in zero-cost launch. Later, opt-in per category; no marketing notifications by default. Proposed cap: one general digest/day at most, user-configurable session reminders, and an independently controlled urgent confirmed-update category. Caps are maximums, not quotas to fill.

Use quiet hours and user timezone. Don't batch delayed breaking alerts after a long outage. Correction alerts should reach the affected audience when feasible, but old system notifications cannot always be recalled. “We miss you” guilt messages and lost-streak warnings are out of scope.

Email is optional later, requiring real consent and unsubscribe support. Start with RSS/manual research follow-up rather than a paid email automation service. Never send to addresses collected for a different purpose.

## 11. Analytics specification

Events to define before implementation:

| Event | Useful properties | What it does not prove |
|---|---|---|
| `moment_opened` | content ID/revision, entry route, depth | Understanding |
| `explanation_completed` | explicit completion or documented heuristic | Reading every word |
| `evidence_opened` | evidence ID, source class | Agreement with conclusion |
| `depth_changed` | old/new mode | User expertise |
| `scenario_compared` | model version, preset ID | Correct prediction |
| `item_saved` | content type, local/synced | Long-term retention |
| `share_initiated` | format, spoiler mode | Message actually sent |
| `shared_link_opened` | coarse campaign/content token | Unique new person without consented dedupe |
| `widget_configured` | widget type, size mode | Ongoing widget usage |
| `correction_viewed` | publication revision | All prior readers informed |

Do not store free-text searches, full URLs containing private parameters, sensitive profile data or device fingerprints by default. Define a schema allowlist. In the ₹0 pilot, use consenting testers, local event exports and qualitative sessions; production analytics infrastructure is optional. Never report measured retention if no reliable consented identity/cohort exists.

## 12. Experiment backlog

| Priority | Hypothesis | Compare | Primary measure | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Direct example beats setup | Example-first vs optional interests-first | Comprehension + first useful journey | No spoiler exposure |
| 2 | Plain labels improve trust | “Official / Our analysis” vs less explicit layout | Correct identification | No increase in task time/confusion |
| 3 | Depth control bridges audiences | Inline switch vs separate analyst route | Successful task by segment | Source access stays easy |
| 4 | Moment cards earn sharing | Insight-first card vs headline-only | Recipient useful journeys | Accuracy and spoiler safety |
| 5 | Bounded brief improves return | Five moments vs ten moments | Next-weekend return | Completion and satisfaction |
| 6 | Widget is useful beyond novelty | Schedule widget onboarding offered after value | Continued voluntary use | Battery/staleness complaints |
| 7 | Scenario challenges teach | Static explanation vs curated choice/reveal | Correct concept explanation | No false certainty |
| 8 | Following aids relevance | Explicit follows vs editorial-only home | Useful content discovery | Diversity, filter bubbles |

Small pilot: qualitative comparison and interviews, not underpowered significance claims. At larger sample size, preregister hypothesis, unit of assignment, minimum detectable effect, duration, sample-size calculation, guardrails and stopping rule. Avoid repeatedly checking p-values and declaring a winner early. Randomize by user, not page request, and account for race-weekend variation. No invented lift percentages.

## 13. Validation gates

Gate 1: five of eight usability participants can complete all critical tasks without prompting, and no unresolved official-versus-analysis confusion. This is a formative threshold; fix issues even if the aggregate passes.

Gate 2: at least five of the initial 12–20 testers voluntarily return across two covered race weekends, with qualitative explanations of value. This validates further learning, not PMF.

Gate 3: three consecutive larger cohorts show stable/improving race-weekend return; substantive correction rate and editorial effort stay within agreed limits. Decide sample size based on desired precision before interpreting rates.

Gate 4: unsolicited shares bring users who actually complete useful journeys. Paid acquisition remains deferred until retained value and affordable marginal costs are demonstrated.

If visitors open but don't understand: fix explanation/UI. If they understand but don't return: investigate recurring need. If retained users don't share: make the share artifact useful. If shares convert poorly: fix the destination. A reward does not fix an unhelpful product.

## 14. Monetization, later

Operating mode: design from scratch. Current price, paid conversion, margin, LTV and willingness-to-pay are unknown. Treat SaaS pricing frameworks as a structure, not evidence that fans should pay enterprise-style tiers.

Value metric: access to advanced saved analysis/workspaces, not per headline or per source click. Initial packaging hypothesis:

- Free: core explanations, evidence, schedule, basic widgets, saved items and shared stories.
- Supporter/Analyst, only later: richer personal workspaces, additional validated scenarios, export conveniences and optional visual customization.
- Creator offering, much later: specifically licensed original explanatory assets/tools, not resale of underlying third-party data.

Do not charge for corrections, truth labels, basic privacy or the ability to cancel. No arbitrary three-tier enterprise plan. No “most popular” badge without real evidence. Annual commitments should not be pushed before reliability/seasonality is understood.

Research: ask qualified retained users what they'd lose without the product, what they already pay for and what paid feature they would actually use. Use feature trade-offs and clearly labelled concept-price tests. Survey sample size should match precision/segmentation needs; 30 responses do not magically establish a valid price. Never charge a fake-door test or imply an unavailable subscription exists.

Cost model: contribution per paid user = collected revenue minus applicable taxes/fees/refunds, marginal delivery/model costs and allocated rights costs. Calculate editorial labour separately and include it in sustainability planning. No LTV/CAC claims until retention and costs are measured. Play digital subscriptions require review of applicable billing rules before implementation. [Play payments policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738).

## 15. Community and reputation

V1 feedback is a report-issue/contact route, not open comments. Later structured discussion would require code of conduct, reporting/blocking, moderation ownership, appeals, spam controls and handling abuse against drivers/teams/users. Never create fake community activity or seed fabricated testimonials.

Public editorial principles: admit uncertainty; link primary evidence; distinguish news from opinion; correct promptly; disclose material sponsorship; do not let sponsors alter technical conclusions. Trust compounds slowly and is easily lost by one confident false accusation.
